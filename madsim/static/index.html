What is this?

What happens when more countries get the bomb?

Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD) is the idea that if two countries can each wipe the other out, neither will attack first, because the counter-attack would be certain. It kept the peace between the US and USSR for decades.

This simulator asks a simple question: does that logic still work when there are 3, 10, or 30 nuclear countries? Each country is run by an AI leader (Jev) who sees the world and picks a move every year. We replay history many times and count how often the world ends.

1 · Set the number of countriesUse the slider or a preset on the right.
2 · Jev plays every leaderEach year every leader chooses: hold, talk, build, disarm or fire.
3 · We count the endingsHow often does someone fire? How often does everyone die?

Why more countries means more danger

  1. Nobody is perfectly safe. Even a calm leader has some tiny chance each year of firing: a false alarm, a crisis, a bluff gone wrong.
  2. Tiny chances add up. If each of n leaders has a 1% chance of firing this year, the chance that at least one does is about n%. With 30 leaders that's ~26% per year, not 1%.
  3. MAD guarantees the counter-attack. That is what stops rational leaders, but it also means one launch becomes a chain reaction, especially through alliances.
  4. So the world survives 2 well, and ∞ never. The right formula is below: as n grows, the chance nobody ever fires shrinks to zero.
P(nobody fires in T years) ≤ (1 − p)n·T → 0 as n → ∞
